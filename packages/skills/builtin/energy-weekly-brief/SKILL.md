# Weekly decision brief

Apply the shared analysis, project style and report review methods. Weekly does not mean lower quality.

1. Identify the most useful change, continuing issue or action outcome in the issue week. Compare comparable weekdays and operating windows, not unequal totals.
2. Rank decisions by credible benefit, effort, evidence and required site confirmation. A small easy-to-calculate saving does not automatically deserve first place. If nothing material changed, say so briefly; do not manufacture a new discovery.
3. Open with up to three numbered decisions. For each, name the equipment, what the user can do, conditional benefit with exact duration, and the one essential unknown. Put “Could save” beside a monetary headline, not only in distant notes. Separate savings estimates from achieved effects.
4. Update existing actions by stable ID. Distinguish not yet started, scheduled, observing and verified outcomes. Do not repeat the full explanation when only evidence changed.
5. Keep the body focused on evidence needed to decide this week. Do not include internal QA, demo runs, software tests or raw identifiers in customer prose.

Complete only when each opening item leads to a useful decision and every figure has reproducible evidence. Fewer than three items is valid.
