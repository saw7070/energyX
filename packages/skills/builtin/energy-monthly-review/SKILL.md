# Monthly management review

Apply the same shared analysis and review standards as a weekly report. More pages are not a quality target.

1. Establish the month's recorded consumption, cost where supported and coverage. Compare with a previous comparable month only when available. Clearly distinguish partial-month evidence; never fill missing days by unsupported extrapolation.
2. Explain major drivers and persistent opportunities across spaces and named equipment. Use full history for context, while keeping issue totals within the month. Do not concatenate weekly reports.
3. Review actions: what was actually reported done, what subsequent data shows, what remains uncertain and what needs a management decision. A user completion statement is not causal proof of savings.
4. Select up to three management priorities. Rank credible benefit against effort, operational constraints and confidence. Keep low-value opportunities subordinate. State the next decision and verification plan.
5. Label retrospective month-specific findings separately from current homepage priorities. Do not replace newer evidence with older monthly observations. Exclude QA/demo/software-validation history from customer text.

## Reading for decision makers

Write the month as a story a manager with no energy background can follow and act on:

- **Month at a glance** first: in two or three plain sentences, what the month cost (SGD), how that compares with the previous month (or say there is no comparable month yet), the main reason, and the decision that matters most.
- **How the month went**: a daily chart for the whole month with weekends, public holidays and planned closures marked, and one sentence on what stands out.
- **Where the money went and why**: by area and type of use, then the few devices or circuits that drove the result, with what was left on after working hours.
- **What changed**: new issues, ongoing ones and verified improvements since last month, each tied to evidence.
- **Decisions needed**: a short table with each decision, the options, the recommended option, the condition-bound yearly benefit, the effort and who should act.

Give each point one chart with a plain-English takeaway above it. Explain any unavoidable term the first time it appears. Keep calculations and methods in expandable detail.

Complete only when the report answers how the month performed, why it matters, and what to prioritize next. If history or action observations are insufficient, explain that limit without inventing a comparison or outcome.
