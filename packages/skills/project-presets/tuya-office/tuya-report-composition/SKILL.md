# Tuya Report Composition

Apply the shared report-presentation.md reader-first structure. Use the separate energy-report-investigation method for discovery and calculations. Read current project-context.md for address, rooms, relative layout, source status and scenario assumptions, and project-configuration.json for published counting and meter relationships. Keep those facts out of this reusable presentation method.

Use a restrained editorial layout, dark ink, Tuya orange, warm figure backgrounds, thin rules and clear chart labels. Prefer supplied business device/circuit names to opaque IDs. Render a useful room/DB schematic when the current project context contains layout evidence; preserve its reference-derived status and distinguish physical spaces from analytical subgroups. Recompute every energy or cost label for the selected period.

The earlier report's property map, load/cost comparison, internal benchmarks, hourly profile and circuit investigation are a toolbox, not five compulsory chapters. Start with important conclusions; choose and combine supporting sections to fit the strongest current questions. Repeated unchanged detail can move to notes. A new supported finding can displace an old section; there is no inherited word or page budget.

Use short decision headlines above the evidence, with warm white space and generous chart labels. Merge continuous-load share, off-hours consumption and its conditional control scenario when they support the same site check. Put the schematic beside the relevant spatial analysis or immediately after the briefing; mark only source-supported relationships. Show business circuit names from the published configuration. If the name is still generic, keep the honest available name and record the naming gap; do not invent equipment identity. Consolidate repeated occupancy/switchability caveats into the affected analysis and scenario conditions, while retaining the uncertainty next to the claim.

Give internal comparison lines and compact supporting tables when useful. Label weekday/weekend sample counts and modelled baseline separately. Prefer a precise current-period circuit contribution over a historical percentage. Use one actionable priority table and conditional cost/savings scenarios; avoid duplicating the same recommendations as a second checklist.
